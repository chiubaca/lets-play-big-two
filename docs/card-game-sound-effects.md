# Sound effects for Big Two

Researched 11 October 2026 against official asset, creator-upload, license, and
download pages. **22 candidates were downloaded:** 18 selections from Kenney's
Casino Audio and Interface Sounds, plus four bell/vibrophone files by n4.
Unused candidates and duplicate research copies were removed after selection.
See [the source record](assets/sound-effects/README.md) for retained runtime assets,
licenses, and download provenance. The research audio was decoded/validated, but not
listened to; suggested uses are candidates to audition, not claims about how the
recordings sound. Anonymous HTTP HEAD checks also verified Kenney UI Audio,
OpenGameArt's card ZIP, and one Sonniss ZIP without fetching their bodies.

## Recommendation

Start with **Kenney Casino Audio** for tactile card movement, then audition
**Kenney Interface Sounds** for a quiet selection/deselection pair. For the turn
cue, try **n4's CC0 bell/vibrophone files** or HaelDB's explicitly listed
pass-turn notification. These asset pages all label their downloads CC0.[1], [2], [3], [4], [5]
CC0 permits modification, distribution, and commercial use without asking
permission; OpenGameArt's license explanation explicitly confirms no credit is
required. Keep voluntary credits and provenance anyway.[7], [8]

The intended palette is **paper flicks and soft table taps, not casino jackpots
or constant arcade bloops**. Choose a small, coherent subset rather than mixing
every pack. Actual quality, harshness, noise, and relative loudness still need
listening tests.

## Other candidates and licensing distinctions

- **[Sonniss GameAudioGDC archive][11]:** a legitimate no-account fallback with
  public ZIPs and official torrents; commercial use and no attribution are
  explicitly allowed. An anonymous HEAD on the [2024 part 1 ZIP][12] returned
  200 and a content length of 2,925,157,094 bytes: an unnecessarily large first
  download for a handful of UI sounds. Use the archive's official track lists
  before choosing a part; no specific card/chime recording was confirmed here.
  **Not CC0.** Current EULA v2.0, effective 27 August 2026, allows use/modification
  in finished games but restricts supplying the sounds as sounds, including
  modified sounds and asset packs/project templates; team sharing is allowed.
  It also prohibits AI development/training uses. For a new download, the
  license published on the download date governs, even if the ZIP contains an
  older copy. Avoid putting its raw files in a public source/asset repository
  without permission.[13]
- **[Freesound FAQ][14]:** original sound downloads require a registered,
  logged-in account, so it does not meet the no-account preference. Licenses are
  per recording: CC0, CC BY, CC BY-NC, and some legacy Sampling+ material exist.
  CC BY permits commercial use but requires appropriate credit, a license link,
  and disclosure of changes, and prohibits additional legal/technical
  restrictions; CC BY-NC is unsuitable for this commercial-use shortlist.[14], [15]
- **Pixabay:** its [official license-summary page][16] returned HTTP 403 during
  this research. Current license and sound-download/account behavior were not
  verified; it is intentionally not shortlisted rather than assumed CC0 or
  account-free.
- **OpenGameArt is not uniformly CC0.** Use the specific creator pages above,
  not a collection-wide assumption. Its FAQ also warns that preview audio may
  have a different license from downloadable assets: do not extract the preview
  as a substitute for the licensed download.[8]

## Proposed sound design

The following are design recommendations, not measured properties of the packs.

| Event              | Treatment to aim for                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Select card        | Tiny dry paper flick or muted tick, roughly 80–150 ms. No hover sound.                                                                                        |
| Deselect card      | Same material, slightly lower/softer variant; distinguish it without sounding like an error.                                                                  |
| Play one card      | Soft slide/tap, roughly 150–250 ms, aligned with the accepted play landing.                                                                                   |
| Play several cards | One slightly fuller card-group sound, not five simultaneous loud hits.                                                                                        |
| Deal               | A brief rhythmic paper sequence; if individual cards animate, keep ticks quiet and cap overlapping voices.                                                    |
| Your turn          | One warm bell/mallet-like note or restrained two-note cue, roughly 400–800 ms after trimming; quick soft attack and short decay, not an alarm or win fanfare. |

Keep selection quieter than play, and the turn cue noticeable but nonabrasive.
Trim dead air, soften sharp attacks/high-frequency ringing, and audition repeated
actions on headphones and phone speakers at low volume. Use only a few related
variants with modest pitch variation. Trigger once per meaningful state change,
not every render/reconnect, and never make louder sounds a reward for rapid taps.

## Mute and accessibility

- Provide a persistent, clearly labelled **Sound effects on/off** control with
  keyboard/screen-reader access and an announced state; remember the choice.
  Consider separate interaction and turn-cue volume controls plus a master mute.
- Never require the chime to understand whose turn it is: retain visible
  **Your turn** text and a suitable screen-reader announcement; selection must
  also have a non-audio state. WCAG's sensory-characteristics criterion says
  operating instructions must not rely solely on sound or other sensory
  characteristics.[17]
- Keep the game usable completely muted. Avoid page-load audio, repetitive
  reminders, and cues that compete with screen-reader speech. W3C explains that
  automatic audio can interfere with screen readers; WCAG 1.4.2 specifically
  requires independent stop/pause or volume control when automatic audio lasts
  more than three seconds. Short effects are not all covered by that threshold,
  but a master mute remains the recommendation here.[18]
- For further downloads, preserve the asset page, license/date, creator, and
  selected original filename in a provenance record. For stock-license sources,
  check repository redistribution before committing; CC0 is the simpler choice.
